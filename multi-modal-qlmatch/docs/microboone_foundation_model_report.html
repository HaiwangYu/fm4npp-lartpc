01 / Decision

Yes for a research prototype; incomplete for a full event-selection benchmark.

The MicroBooNE open samples contain enough paired charge and light information to pretrain a reusable detector encoder and fine-tune it with a smaller labeled subset. You can start with reconstructed hits in HDF5, keeping truth labels hidden during pretraining. Learning a representation this way is usually called self-supervised learning: training targets come from the measurements themselves.

Recommended first system: inclusive NoWire HDF5 → sparse wire–time charge encoder with coarse attention + optical-hit tokens → shared latent fusion → hit/slice neutrino tagging and CC electron-versus-muon classification. Compare masked reconstruction and observable-token self-distillation on the same backbone; retain an FM4NPP-style Mamba alternative for scaling. Use the electron-neutrino sample to supplement rare labeled examples after checking overlaps and matching selections.

The release combines simulated BNB neutrino interactions with measured off-beam cosmic activity and detector noise. It is therefore an overlay corpus, not a large collection of fully real neutrino events. That distinction limits claims about performance on beam data. [MicroBooNE release]

Charge + lightNoWire removes wire waveforms, not charge hits or optical measurements.

Dataset facts below are documented. Model configurations, training objectives, and evaluation protocols are recommendations for this project. No training, bulk-event audit, or performance measurement was performed for this report.

Three boundaries that change the experiment

  1. Hit-level cosmic tagging is possible; whole-event negatives are not supplied as a dedicated sample. An overlay readout contains a generated neutrino even when it is outside the active volume or produces little visible activity. “Generated neutrino exists” is consequently a poor binary event target. Start by identifying neutrino-associated charge within each mixed readout. Obtain an independent, trigger-compatible off-beam cosmic-only sample before claiming full cosmic-event rejection.
  2. Flavor labels support CC electron-versus-muon studies. A visible outgoing charged lepton provides flavor evidence in CC interactions. NC interactions generally do not carry comparable event-by-event flavor information. Define a separate NC/other class rather than promise reliable three-flavor identification. This corpus is not a practical ντ-CC benchmark, and charge-sign/ν–anti-ν separation should not be conflated with flavor.
  3. Full raw charge–light modeling needs art/ROOT. Its product list includes mixed raw::RawDigit and raw::OpDetWaveform products, plus original-data and simulated components. Use the combined overlay products for training; selecting simulation-only optical or charge branches would change the domain and can introduce an origin shortcut. Confirm process, module, instance, timestamps, gain streams, and per-file availability before export. [art/ROOT product list]

Timing needs a dedicated audit. The optical tutorial describes a 1,500-sample, roughly 23.4 μs beam-gate recording, with neutrinos expected about 4 μs after its start; flashes integrate roughly 8 μs of light. It plots optical-hit peak times in μs, while the schema leaves “ticks” with a #CHECK note. Treat the tutorial/exporter as evidence to resolve the discrepancy, then verify an actual file and the detector clock configuration. Never equate TPC arrival ticks and PMT emission times. [Optical tutorial] [Exporter]

The short optical window does not cover every cosmic interaction contributing charge to the much longer TPC record. Therefore, missing light is not evidence of missing charge. Include readout-window and availability masks, and avoid a training loss that assumes every charge patch has a directly observed optical counterpart.

Implementation detail to verify: the public exporter associates an OpHit’s sumpe_id with the highest-PE flash, and writes per-flash sum identifiers that reset for each flash. Do not interpret this field as a complete all-flash association map or join it by ID alone. Preserve composite event/flash/channel keys. [Exporter optical loop]

04 / Tokenizer

Use a common token interface with modality-specific measurement adapters.

“Tokenize similarly” should mean that both streams produce local measurement embeddings with position, time, signal strength, sensor identity, and availability. The two measurements have different transfer functions and units, so forcing the same raw numerical encoder or spatial coordinates is an unnecessary constraint. Continuous vectors are already tokens; a discrete vocabulary is optional. In a Panda-style experiment, learned prototypes belong to the self-supervised prediction head; they need not replace the continuous input tokens with a VQ codebook.

token = feature_adapter(modality, signal, width, local_shape)
+ position_encoder(native_geometry, native_time)
+ sensor_embedding + modality_embedding + availability_embedding

charge and light tokens both have dimension d; units remain explicit upstream.

Charge tokenizer: patches of reconstructed wire–time hits Start here

Build small neighborhoods separately in each of the three planes, using physical wire distance and arrival-time separation with a documented conversion scale. A mini-PointNet, small local-attention block, or sparse 2D convolution pools each neighborhood into a token. Retain within-patch offsets and a skip path to individual hits for later segmentation.

Use integral, pulse width, local hit count, and charge-shape statistics as continuous features. Fit signal transforms and normalization on training data only. Preserve total amplitude information in an additional feature if local normalization would erase energy cues. Avoid summing the three planes as three independent energy deposits.

A wire–time hit is a projection, not a 3D point. Learn cross-plane relationships through wire-intersection geometry and compatible drift coordinates. If a reconstructed 3D point cloud is preferred, export and validate it from art/ROOT; do not manufacture XYZ coordinates from one plane, use simulated energy-deposit positions as inputs, or assume the public HDF5 schema supplies reconstructed XYZ hits.

Light tokenizer: optical hits first, flashes as a complementary view

Encode one PMT pulse or a short channel-time neighborhood per token. Supply sensor geometry, emission-time coordinate after clock validation, width, and a transformed signal magnitude. An optical token identifies the sensor collecting photons; its coordinates are not the interaction vertex. Light propagates nonlocally, so fusion must learn a many-to-many relation.

Keep all available optical hits, including those outside the strongest flash. A separate flash view can encode the 32-PMT calibrated PE pattern and flash timing into a small set of tokens. This is a useful low-cost baseline, but hit and flash representations derive from the same light measurements: do not let one reveal the other’s masked target. Preserve calibration conventions rather than mixing uncalibrated hit PE and calibrated flash PE as interchangeable quantities.

Use fixed geometry-defined mask regions and reconstruct occupancy as well as signal, or explicitly define a narrower attribute-only masking task.

Resolve the transfer claim using the primary paper. Seminar slide 29 describes the same weights transferring across detectors. Panda Diplomacy instead states independent pretraining for each detector, explicitly excludes a single shared backbone in Appendix C.1, and leaves cross-detector weight transfer untested in §4. The established result is portability of the training recipe. Joint training and transferred weights require new evidence. [Paper §4] [Appendix C] [Slide 29]

Do not infer that MAE or DINO is universally superior. Seminar slide 42 shows a 7.24M-parameter MAE k-NN macro-F1 of 0.54, while the DINO curve uses a 0.47M model. Architecture size and training settings differ. This preliminary comparison motivates a matched-backbone, matched-budget objective study; it does not settle the choice. [Slide 42]

Position the contribution around paired measurements.

Proposed research contribution: learn from paired MicroBooNE charge and light without truth during pretraining, using a common token contract that respects different geometry, clocks, and coverage; then demonstrate improved low-label neutrino-origin and CC-flavor performance. Compare against strong charge-only pretrained encoders and conventional fusion. A shared embedding dimension by itself is not sufficient evidence of useful multimodal learning.

In the supplied works, 3D charge-cloud learning and separately trained optical-detector models do not establish joint charge–light learning in a single LArTPC readout. Treat that as a gap to investigate within this reference set, not an established literature-wide priority claim. Likewise, “sensor-level” in a 3D energy/charge-cloud paper does not imply modeling raw TPC electronics and PMT waveforms.

Reproducibility: Panda’s linked repository is the natural V1 implementation starting point. The V2 manuscript says full configurations/training code are planned for an extended update; verify availability before planning exact reproduction. [V2 Appendix C.2]

Concrete pilot configuration: width 256, 4 local charge blocks, 2 optical blocks, 128 shared latent queries, 6 latent processing blocks, and lightweight 2-block decoders. Start with a bounded 1,000–4,000 charge-patch budget; profile real occupancy before fixing it. Use spatially stratified pooling rather than retaining only the highest-charge hits. These numbers are engineering starting points, not validated hyperparameters or hardware guarantees.

For fixed latent count M, fusion scales roughly as O(NM + M²) per cross-attention/latent stage rather than O(N²) over every hit. This is the relevant idea from Perceiver IO. Add per-modality normalization or balanced pooling so numerous charge tokens do not drown out light.

Additional precedents and implementation boundaries

  • PoLAr-MAE is the closest LArTPC reference: masked point patches plus energy prediction, with strong label efficiency for track/shower segmentation. Its simulated 3D charge setting does not establish performance on MicroBooNE wire projections or paired PMT light.
  • FM4NPP uses serialized sPHENIX spacepoints, Mamba2, a geometric neighbor prediction objective, and frozen-backbone adapters. Transfer the scalable sequence/adaptation strategy; redesign the collider radial ordering and neighbor target for wire-plane geometry and distributed cosmic origins.
  • Particle Transformer motivates pairwise attention biases. MAE and Point-MAE motivate masked local-token learning.
  • Jets-to-neutrinos transfer work finds gains on MINERvA tasks from particle-physics pretrained models. Existing weights are a worthwhile baseline, but this is not evidence that raw MicroBooNE charge and light share the same input semantics.

Detector coordinates, boundaries, wire directions, drift field, and PMT coverage break generic rotation symmetry. Do not enforce unrestricted 3D rotation invariance or import a point-cloud augmentation recipe without checking its validity for this detector.

07 / Learning

Compare reconstruction and local self-distillation before combining them.

Use the large training partition without neutrino truth, particle categories, Pandora scores, or truth-selected crops. Fine-tuning can later reveal labels for a small subset of those same training events. Validation and test readouts must remain outside pretraining for a conventional held-out evaluation.

Self-distillation experiment: dense correspondence before event alignment

Panda uses an EMA teacher and per-point prototype targets, avoiding a single global class token for mixed interactions. Its V1 masked-point prediction and V2 observable-point distillation are distinct objectives; do not silently merge them in a reproduction. [Panda §3.3] [V2 §2.2]

Proposed MicroBooNE adaptation: the teacher sees a broader measured view, while the student sees activity-based local crops, block removal, and occasional optical dropout. Predict teacher assignments only at retained charge or optical locations present in both views, mapping indices through all crops and coordinate transforms. Keep separate modality prototype heads initially; forcing a charge deposition and a remote PMT pulse to have identical assignments has no natural one-to-one target.

Ldistill = mean over corresponding visible locations i:
CE(stop_gradient(p_teacher(i)), p_student(i))
teacher weights ← EMA(student weights)

Run MAE-only and distillation-only first; hybrid losses are a third ablation.

Use asymmetric teacher/student temperatures and balanced teacher assignments, as in Panda’s Sinkhorn-based collapse prevention; EMA alone is insufficient. [Panda §3.3.3] Monitor prototype occupancy, feature covariance/effective rank, and downstream frozen-probe quality. Keep energy-sensitive measurements and native geometry available to downstream heads; augmentations should not erase the features needed for flavor or timing. If teacher inputs include both modalities and student inputs omit light, evaluate whether the distilled charge features gain useful interaction information under independent event splits.

Use modest channel/patch dropout and realistic signal perturbations, with missing-modality training so the model can operate without light. Any synthetic time shift must be consistent across clocks and readout-window boundaries. Do not freely rescale signal magnitude when it encodes energy, crop using a true vertex, or remove the neutrino with a truth mask to fabricate clean cosmic negatives: these transformations can change the task or create a detectable artifact.

Judge a representation by downstream transfer as well as reconstruction. Lower masked loss can mean better noise modeling rather than better neutrino recognition. Keep a charge-only checkpoint, a light-only checkpoint, and a joint checkpoint trained with comparable budgets.

08 / Adaptation

Downstream targets and heads must match the decision level.

A. Identify neutrino activity among cosmics

First target: a per-hit neutrino-association probability. Decode the shared representation back to local charge hits and apply a small MLP or sparse decoder. The collaboration’s hit-labeling tutorial takes the largest associated simulated deposit and assigns hits without a matched simulated particle to the cosmic category. That is a useful operational target, but an unmatched hit can include noise or failed association; real cosmic ancestry is not fully labeled. [Hit-labeling tutorial]

Call the benchmark “neutrino-associated versus unmatched background hits” until label quality is audited. Mixed simulation/data contributions need special care: do not assume the provided simulation deposit fractions are calibrated fractions of all observed charge. Establish ambiguous-hit handling and thresholds against the overlay/backtracking implementation.

Second target: candidate/slice selection. Pool hit features for reconstruction-defined candidate groups, fuse with the full available light context, and apply a sigmoid score head. Derive labels from neutrino-hit overlap with stated purity/completeness requirements. Pandora groups offer a convenient first benchmark, but report how often the true neutrino was absent or fragmented before the classifier ran. An eventual interaction-query decoder can learn grouping and scoring together.

Third target: whole-event neutrino selection. Use a pooled/event-query head after obtaining realistic cosmic-only negatives and specifying “visible in-fiducial-volume neutrino” as the signal. Neutrino interactions outside the active volume may be a separate background. A binary classifier on nu_pdg != 0 across this release does not solve the desired task.

B. Identify flavor

Pool the selected interaction’s charge features, retain small-scale shower-start and track-end information, fuse light context, and train a compact softmax head. Begin with CC νe-like versus CC νμ-like targets using abs(nu_pdg) = 12 versus 14 and is_cc = 1. For a realistic selection chain, include NC/other and a reject/background option rather than forcing every candidate into one of the two flavors.

Light primarily helps associate charge with an in-time interaction; detailed charge topology and calorimetry are likely to carry most flavor information. Treat this as a hypothesis and test it. NC events, photon showers, cosmic muons, and cosmic Michel electrons are important confusion cases. Use the reconstructed selected candidate at evaluation; a truth-selected neutrino-only crop yields an easier oracle benchmark that should be labeled separately.

Fine-tune in four modes: frozen encoder plus linear/MLP probe, frozen encoder plus learned adapter, LoRA on attention layers plus a small head, and partial/full encoder fine-tuning. Use class-balanced sampling for rare CC electron events in training, then evaluate and calibrate with representative priors. Do not infer neutrino charge sign simply from electron/muon flavor classification.

For learned interaction grouping, compare a query-based mask/class head and a small coarse-to-fine GNN. Add optical context to the interaction query or candidate node before its neutrino/flavor decision. The pretrained encoder need not solve grouping itself. Supervise only the instances the release can truth-match; unmatched real cosmic hits do not supply complete cosmic-particle instance labels.

09 / Evidence

Make label efficiency and multimodal benefit measurable.

QuestionRequired comparison / metric
Does pretraining help?Same architecture from scratch versus pretrained; label budgets such as 100, 1,000, 10,000, and full training events. Keep labels, tuning budget, and supervised compute comparable; also report the additional pretraining compute.
Which objective helps?On the same input encoder, compare MAE, observable-token distillation, and a hybrid at matched parameter/data/compute budgets. Separate teacher cost, decoder cost, and head capacity from the objective itself.
Which architecture helps?Compare the sparse 2D attention/fusion encoder with an FM4NPP-style serialized SSM under the same data and downstream protocol. Treat a 3D Panda reproduction as a separate input-representation comparison.
Does light add useful information?Charge-only, light-only, late fusion, and joint fusion. Remove light, shuffle light between occupancy/run-matched events, and degrade sensor coverage at evaluation.
Is cosmic rejection meaningful?Signal efficiency at fixed background acceptance, PR-AUC, and residual background rates under stated priors. For hit/slice tagging, include charge-weighted purity/completeness and event-level recovery.
Is flavor learning robust?Per-class efficiency/purity, macro-F1, confusion matrix, calibration; performance versus energy, vertex, containment, and cosmic occupancy. Report both oracle and full-chain results if both are used.
Is the encoder reusable?Add track/shower segmentation, interaction vertexing, or energy regression with a separate small head. Two related classification heads alone provide limited evidence of broad transfer.

Leakage and domain-shift controls

  • Split readouts before patching. Keep all views, crops, waveforms, and tokenizations of a readout in one partition. Deduplicate across samples and formats using provenance, not only row numbers. Scope run/subrun/event keys by production where needed.
  • Audit original cosmic-frame reuse. Different overlays may reuse the same off-beam data. Group by original frame if provenance permits, or use content similarity and stronger run/subrun grouping as diagnostics. If the release lacks enough provenance, disclose that independence is not proven.
  • Keep truth and teacher outputs out of baseline inputs. Flavor, interaction type, true vertices/energies, deposit ancestry, Pandora neutrino/flash-match scores, IDs, filenames, and source labels can leak targets or provenance. IDs remain useful for joins and splits. Benchmark a separate reconstruction-assisted variant if Pandora features are intentionally used.
  • Prevent source shortcuts. Restrict comparison to common fiducial selections and inspect energy/occupancy distributions; test a source-prediction probe. Class weighting cannot fix mismatched geometric support by itself.
  • Separate overlay performance from deployment. Evaluate on independent beam-off and beam-on data when available; test gain/noise/timing perturbations and alternate simulation conditions. Truth in overlay provides training labels, not a guarantee of real-neutrino generalization.
  • Report uncertainty by event. Bootstrap readouts or provenance groups, rather than treating millions of correlated hits as independent observations. Use multiple seeds for small label-budget experiments.

Charge–light association is physically motivated: MicroBooNE’s Wire-Cell work uses many-to-many matching to reject cosmic muons. It supports the fusion hypothesis, but its reported reconstruction performance cannot be assigned to this proposed network. [Wire-Cell charge–light matching]

10 / Execution

A staged pilot with clear decision points.

1

Audit one inclusive NoWire shard.

Verify event grouping, composite joins, timing units, optical coverage, missing fields, calibration conventions, hit/flash multiplicities, and class/volume counts. Reproduce one event display and the collaboration’s charge–light comparison. Save a schema/units/provenance manifest and immutable train/validation/test assignments.

2

Build minimal supervised baselines and a hit-based encoder.

Begin with roughly 10,000 training readouts for engineering. Compare a sparse charge model and a small charge+flash model, then implement the common token interface. Use all measured hits, not only Pandora’s neutrino-selected particles. Measure memory, throughput, and truncation effects on small signals.

3

Compare MAE and self-distillation, then test transfer.

Train both objectives on the same pilot encoder and data with truth hidden. Use identical low-label subsets for frozen probes, adapters/LoRA, and from-scratch baselines. Compare charge-only and charge+light. Advance only if gains survive source and timing controls; add hybrid or cross-modal prediction losses afterward.

4

Scale the corpus and add the rare flavor sample.

Use the inclusive NoWire training partition, then add deduplicated electron-neutrino data with matched selection. Bucket variable-length events and create compact token caches. Budget at least the published 226 GB for both NoWire samples plus actual portal-size differences, conversion space, checkpoints, and caches. Benchmark throughput before estimating GPU-hours.

5

Resolve the event-selection data gap; decide on raw modeling.

Request trigger-compatible independent off-beam readouts and suitable beam-data validation access. Ask about original cosmic-frame provenance, detector-clock/calibration metadata, optical gain mapping, and availability of photon-response resources. If waveform-level learning remains important, validate a small art/ROOT export before downloading terabytes.

Project recommendation: proceed with a “MicroBooNE joint charge–light self-supervised encoder” pilot, with Panda-style distillation and masked reconstruction as primary objective comparisons. Demonstrate gains over charge-only pretraining on hit/slice neutrino tagging and CC flavor, then expand tasks and domains before making a broad foundation-model claim. The main bottleneck is a trustworthy benchmark and input contract.
